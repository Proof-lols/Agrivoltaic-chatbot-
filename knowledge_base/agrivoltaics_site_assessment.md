# Agrivoltaics Site Assessment — Core Reference

## Parcel Scale & Layout
Operational agrivoltaic arrays typically deploy on parcels between 5 and 500 acres. Row spacing must accommodate farm machinery (minimum 20–30 ft for mid-size tractors) while preserving solar access factors above 0.85 for fixed-tilt systems.

## Soil Biology Considerations
Loam to sandy-loam soils with pH 6.0–7.2 support diverse crop rotations under partial shade. Organic matter above 3% improves moisture retention beneath elevated panels. Compaction from construction traffic reduces root-zone porosity—require controlled traffic plans.

## Microclimate Under Arrays
Partial shade reduces peak evapotranspiration by 15–30% depending on panel height and transparency. Frost risk may increase in low-lying inter-row zones; monitor differential soil temperatures. Wind corridors between rows can accelerate crop desiccation in arid regions.

## Capital Benchmarks
Elevated agrivoltaic structures cost $1.20–$2.50 per watt DC installed, excluding land prep. USDA REAP grants may offset 25% of eligible renewable energy costs for qualifying agricultural producers. Payback periods range 7–14 years with net-metering export credits.

## Shade-Tolerant Cultivars
Leafy greens (lettuce, spinach), certain forages (alfalfa under 30% shade), and berries show agronomic tolerance to intermittent shading. Grain crops generally require higher photosynthetically active radiation—limit shade fractions below 20% for corn and wheat.

## Panel Geometry
Fixed-tilt azimuth aligned to true south (northern hemisphere) with tilt equal to latitude ±5° balances annual yield. Single-axis tracking increases solar harvest 15–25% but raises structural costs and shading dynamics—coordinate tracking algorithms with crop sensitivity windows.
